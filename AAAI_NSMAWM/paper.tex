\begin{abstract}
  Neuro-symbolic world models improve long-horizon reliability by injecting partial symbolic transition knowledge --- rules for spatial consistency, object persistence, or action-conditioned effects --- into an otherwise learned multi-agent World Model (MAWM). Existing approaches, including our own prior work, assume this decomposition into symbolically-covered and residual features is handed to the system, either hand-written or produced by a large language model and then manually curated. This leaves open exactly the question that determines whether the approach can scale: can the covered/residual decomposition itself be obtained automatically, and can we trust it? We answer with a falsification-based discovery procedure that treats every candidate invariant as \emph{not yet refuted} rather than \emph{true}: candidates are proposed from an interaction dataset by an enumerative, tree-based, or LLM-guided proposer, certified through active and multi-policy falsification with an explicit statistical validity bound, and injected into the world model with a confidence-weighted, retractable coupling. We give five results characterizing this procedure: a survival-based validity certificate, a search-cost bound governing the expected number of false discoveries, a chaining argument from discovery validity to rollout fidelity, a degradation bound under policy shift, and --- the paper's central negative result --- a non-identifiability theorem showing that under partial observability, passive discovery can certify invariants that are artifacts of the data-collection policy rather than of the environment, which we show can only be ruled out by active, multi-policy, multi-variant falsification. On GridCraft, Overcooked, Predator--Prey, and SMACv2, evaluated under feature anonymization so that discovery cannot succeed by name-matching and averaged over 5 seeds per condition, a decision-tree proposer recovers $0.837\pm0.039$ of the fidelity gain obtained by hand-written oracle rules and an LLM-guided proposer recovers $0.774\pm0.057$, both ahead of brute-force enumeration ($0.612\pm0.048$), whose far larger candidate count buys no certification-budget advantage but does translate into more low-precision survivors, in line with the precision argument above. The difficulty gradient is consistent with the non-identifiability result: a rule over a latent environment variable never entering any agent's observation is falsely certified under passive falsification alone ($\hat\varepsilon=0.006\pm0.005$) and flagged non-certifiable once active and multi-policy falsification are added, as \autoref{thm:t5} predicts.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Neuro-symbolic learning, World Models, multi-agent, model-based RL, rule discovery, partial observability

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL) supports efficient planning by learning predictive World Models (WMs)~\citep{hafner2018planet,hafner2019dreamer,janner2019mbpo,schrittwieser2020muzero}, but rollout errors compound over long horizons, especially in multi-agent settings with partial observability and combinatorial joint dynamics~\citep{willemsen2021mambpo}. A recent line of neuro-symbolic work addresses this by decomposing the observation into a \emph{symbolically covered} sub-vector, predicted by rules, and a \emph{residual} sub-vector, left to a neural component~\citep{balloch2023neurosymbolicworldmodelsadapting,zhu2022iaem}; we refer to this general recipe as \emph{covered/residual decomposition}. It reduces the hypothesis space the neural model must fit and removes error on structurally predictable transitions.

Every version of this recipe we are aware of, including our own Neuro-Symbolic Multi-Agent World Model (NS-MAWM) framework, treats the decomposition as an input: a domain expert writes the rules, or a large language model proposes them and a human filters the result~\citep{zhou2024walle,athalye2026pixelstopredicates}. This is the single point on which the approach's scalability actually depends, and it is exactly the point at which no existing method offers a guarantee. \emph{Can the covered/residual split be obtained automatically, and can the resulting rules be trusted without a human in the loop?}

We answer this question directly rather than working around it. Our contribution is not another covered/residual injection mechanism --- three such mechanisms already exist and we reuse them --- but a procedure for \emph{discovering} the decomposition itself, together with a certificate of when a discovered invariant can be trusted and when it cannot.

\paragraph{Contribution.} We formalize a \emph{partial invariant} as a triplet $(\varphi, c, \rho)$ --- a feature reading, a precondition, and a state-and-action-indexed transport --- whose validity is a conditional determinism rate rather than a binary truth value (\autoref{sec:invariants}). Invariants are proposed from an interaction dataset by an enumerative, decision-tree, or LLM-guided proposer over a compact catalog of transports (persistence, bounded/clipped motion, delayed copy, and slot permutation), then subjected to a falsification protocol with increasing severity: passive holdout, active exploration toward the invariant's precondition, cross-environment-variant testing, and cross-generator testing. Surviving invariants are injected with a confidence that interpolates between a soft regularizer and a hard constraint, and are \emph{retracted} the moment a counterexample appears during training.

We characterize this procedure with five results (\autoref{sec:theory}): a survival-based validity certificate (\autoref{thm:t1}) whose statistical cost scales with the number of candidates actually proposed rather than the size of the search space (\autoref{thm:t2}), together with a corollary showing that a proposer's expected count of false discoveries is controlled by its \emph{precision}, not by the candidate count alone (\autoref{cor:t2b}) --- the argument for using a parsimonious, high-precision proposer such as an LLM rather than brute-force enumeration; a chaining bound from discovery validity to long-horizon rollout fidelity (\autoref{thm:t3}); a degradation bound under policy shift via a restricted concentrability coefficient (\autoref{thm:t4}); and a non-identifiability theorem (\autoref{thm:t5}) showing that under partial observability, a rule can be a perfectly certified artifact of the data-collection policy rather than a fact about the environment --- and that this failure mode is invisible to any passive certification procedure regardless of sample size. This last result is a negative one, and we treat it as a feature of the paper rather than a limitation to bury: it is the reason the falsification protocol must be active and multi-policy, and it is the reason two invariants with identical training-time validity can behave arbitrarily differently under a shifted policy.

We reuse three neuro-symbolic integration strategies from our prior work --- symbolic consistency regularization, symbolic projection, and residual symbolic dynamics --- but correct a design flaw identified in that line of work: the residual head previously predicted uncovered features without access to the symbolic prediction on covered features, discarding information whenever the two families of features are correlated. We condition the residual head on the covered prediction and turn this correlation into an explicit falsification criterion instead of an unexamined assumption (\autoref{sec:injection}).

Finally, we specify an evaluation protocol (\autoref{sec:exp}) centered on a single comparison: the fraction of the gain obtained by hand-written oracle rules that is recovered by automatically discovered ones, measured under feature anonymization (dimension permutation, name stripping, distractor and constant features) so that discovery cannot succeed by recognizing familiar feature names. We report precision and recall against the oracle rule set, the measured coverage $\hat\kappa$ against the oracle's $\kappa$, the false-discovery and retrogradation rates, and an explicit difficulty gradient in which rules over a latent, never-observed environment variable are uncertifiable under passive falsification alone --- exactly as predicted by \autoref{thm:t5}, and confirmed on GridCraft in \autoref{sec:exp}.

\autoref{sec:related} situates the work; \autoref{sec:background} fixes notation; \autoref{sec:invariants} defines partial invariants and the discovery procedure; \autoref{sec:theory} states the theoretical results; \autoref{sec:injection} covers injection into MAWMs; \autoref{sec:exp} specifies the evaluation protocol; \autoref{sec:conclusion} discusses limitations, chief among them the consequences of \autoref{thm:t5}.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{World models and structured dynamics.} Latent WMs such as PlaNet and Dreamer~\citep{hafner2018planet,hafner2019dreamer,hafner2020dreamerv2,hafner2023dreamerv3} and transformer-based WMs~\citep{chen2022transdreamer,micheli2023transformers} learn all dynamics from data. Structured variants add inductive biases via graph dynamics~\citep{zhang2021worldmodelgraph}, contrastive representations~\citep{kipf2020contrastivelearningstructuredworld}, or compositionality~\citep{zhang2024combo}, but do not separate symbolically predictable structure from residual uncertainty, nor discover such a separation.

\paragraph{Discovering invariants over learned or given variables.} A first family discovers \emph{which transformation} makes dynamics simple, given the variables. Symbolic regression and equation discovery (e.g., SINDy-style methods) assume the relevant state variables are already observed and continuous, which excludes discrete multi-agent features, preconditions, and action-conditioning. ConCerNet~\citep{zhang2023concernet} learns a representation \emph{and} a conserved quantity jointly via contrastive learning, then projects the dynamics model to preserve it, with a proven functional link between the latent representation and the invariant --- the closest existing instance of a discover-then-hard-inject loop, but restricted to continuous global conservation laws, single-agent, and without an action-conditioned or partial-coverage notion. The Invariant Action Effect Model (IAEM)~\citep{zhu2022iaem} represents an action's effect as a residual between neighboring-state representations with a contrastive loss and adaptive weighting for cases where the invariance does not hold --- essentially our covered/residual intuition, but already published, single-agent, and without symbolic extraction or a statistical certificate. Flow Equivariant World Models~\citep{lillemark2026flowm} unify self-motion and external-object motion as one-parameter Lie-group flows in a learned equivariant latent, obtaining strong long-horizon stability under partial observability; our canonical example of a spatial invariant (objects translate opposite to the agent's motion) is an instance of exactly this equivariance class, so any claim of learning \emph{which} transport applies must be positioned against it rather than reproduce it.

\paragraph{Learning symbolic action models over given predicates.} STRIPS/PDDL action-model learning gives the strongest existing guarantees, but over hand-defined predicates and for full-information planning objectives. Safe Action Model (SAM) learning extends to partial observability by only updating rules when a literal is observed before and after an action, with polynomial time and a safety guarantee~\citep{lehiu2024safeactionmodelspo}; Conditional-SAM extends safety guarantees to conditional effects, with a proof that conditional effects can require an exponential number of samples in the worst case~\citep{mordoch2024conditionalsam}; a probabilistic alternative infers preconditions and effects from noisy traces via graphical-model inference~\citep{lamanna2024nolam}. These methods assume discrete states, deterministic effects, hand-defined predicates, and target complete symbolic planning domains rather than a residual neural model. From Pixels to Predicates~\citep{athalye2026pixelstopredicates} uses a pretrained vision-language model to propose a large predicate vocabulary and an optimization pass to select a compact subset --- the same propose-many/select-few schema we use for our LLM proposer, but for single-agent robotic planning with full predicate-level abstraction, not partial coverage of a learned latent multi-agent WM.

\paragraph{LLM-guided rule induction for world models.} WALL-E and WALL-E 2.0~\citep{zhou2024walle,zhou2025walle2} induce, update, and prune symbolic rules gradient-free by comparing explored trajectories against world-model predictions, then compose the resulting rules with an LLM-based world model for Minecraft- and ALFWorld-style agents, pruning by maximum coverage. This is the closest system-level precedent to our discovery loop. It differs on every axis relevant to our claim: their world model \emph{is} an LLM operating over text-described, single-agent environments, whereas ours is a latent WM trained on continuous or vectorial multi-agent observations under a Dec-POMDP; their rules are invoked by function call at prediction time rather than injected into training as a soft, hard, or residual bias; and induce/prune is heuristic, without falsification, a validity bound, or a treatment of partial observability. Our non-identifiability result (\autoref{thm:t5}) applies to their setting as much as to ours and is, to our knowledge, not addressed there.

\paragraph{Evaluating world models.} WM evaluation typically emphasizes predictive accuracy, reconstruction loss, or downstream reward, though recent work calls for broader diagnostics of generative models and world models~\citep{Duan_2025_ICCV,Delvecchio2025p1157}. Multi-agent evaluation in particular rarely measures semantic validity --- rule violations, logical consistency, robustness under structured constraints --- which makes it difficult to distinguish a model that is locally accurate from one that is semantically coherent over a long rollout. $\mathrm{RVR}_{\mathrm{pre}}$, $\mathrm{RVR}_{\mathrm{post}}$, and the correction magnitude (\autoref{sec:injection}) are our instantiation of this broader point: a raw neural MAWM can post a low one-step prediction loss while still violating discovered rules on $15$--$18\%$ of covered-feature transitions (\autoref{tab:injection}), a gap invisible to prediction loss alone and directly measured only once semantic validity is tracked as its own quantity.

\paragraph{Non-identifiability under partial data coverage.} \autoref{thm:t5}'s impossibility result is an instance, for invariant discovery, of a phenomenon studied under different names elsewhere: offline RL requires a concentrability or coverage condition on the training distribution for value-function guarantees to transfer to a target policy, and this condition cannot be dispensed with in general~\citep{chen2019information}; off-policy evaluation under confounders unobserved by the acting agent is similarly non-identifiable without further assumptions or robust worst-case bounds~\citep{namkoong2020offpolicy}. We do not claim the underlying phenomenon as a contribution; \autoref{thm:t7}'s positive counterpart --- coverage of the deployment policy by the falsification-gate family restores a finite concentrability-style ratio --- is the same style of fix used in that literature, transposed to rule discovery and made concrete as an operational falsification protocol (F1--F4) rather than a data-collection assumption stated and left unverified.

\paragraph{Gap.} No existing method jointly (i) discovers which observation features of a learned multi-agent latent WM are symbolically predictable and which are irreducibly residual, (ii) certifies the discovered invariants with an explicit, falsification-based statistical guarantee rather than a heuristic induce/prune loop, and (iii) characterizes when such certification is impossible because of partial observability rather than insufficient data. NS-MAWM-style injection~\citep{balloch2023neurosymbolicworldmodelsadapting} and symbolic/organizational biases in MARL more broadly~\citep{hasanbeig2018lcrl,soule2025moisemarl,alshiekh2018shield} supply the injection side; this paper supplies the discovery side and its guarantees.

\section{Background}
\label{sec:background}

Cooperative partially observable MARL is a Decentralized Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (Dec-POMDP)~\citep{oliehoek2016concise,Bernstein2000complexity} $\mathcal{M} = \langle S, \{A_i\}_{i=1}^n, T, R, \{\Omega_i\}_{i=1}^n, O, \gamma \rangle$. Agent $i$'s observation at time $t$ is a structured feature vector $\omega^i_t = (f^i_{t,1},\dots,f^i_{t,n_f})$; joint quantities are written $\omega^j_t=(\omega^1_t,\dots,\omega^n_t)$, $a^j_t=(a^1_t,\dots,a^n_t)$, with joint history $h^j_{t-1}$. A behavioral policy $\pi$ induces a distribution $d^\pi$ over transitions $x=(h^j_{t-1},\omega^j_t,a^j_t,\omega^j_{t+1})$; we write $x^-$ for the part preceding $\omega^j_{t+1}$.

A Multi-Agent World Model (MAWM) predicts $\mathcal{T}^j_\theta: \mathcal{H}^j\times\Omega^j\times A^j\to\mathcal{P}(\Omega^j)$, typically through a latent encoder/decoder pair with recurrent dynamics, following Dreamer- and PlaNet-style recurrent state-space models~\citep{hafner2018planet,hafner2019dreamer,ha2018worldmodels}. Concretely, joint observations are embedded and reconstructed through
\begin{equation}
  z^j_t = \mathrm{Enc}^j(\omega^j_t), \qquad \hat\omega^j_{t+1} = \mathrm{Dec}^j(\hat z^j_{t+1}),
\end{equation}
with a recurrent core updating a deterministic hidden state and, in stochastic variants, a latent state $s^j_t$:
\begin{equation}
  \tilde h_t = f_\theta(\tilde h_{t-1}, z^j_t, a^j_t), \qquad \hat z^j_{t+1} = g_\theta(\tilde h_t),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  s^j_t \sim p_\theta(s^j_t \mid s^j_{t-1}, a^j_{t-1}), \qquad \omega^j_t \sim p_\theta(\omega^j_t \mid s^j_t),
\end{equation}
with approximate inference $q_\phi(s^j_t\mid \omega^j_{\le t}, a^j_{<t})$ as in PlaNet/Dreamer recurrent state-space models~\citep{hafner2018planet,hafner2019dreamer,hafner2020dreamerv2}. Such a MAWM may be centralized, factorized per agent, or relational via attention or graph structure, typically with centralized training and decentralized execution~\citep{oliehoek2016concise,marl-book,wang2022}. Compared with single-agent WMs, MAWMs face combinatorial joint-actions, non-stationarity, and inter-agent dependencies that make long-horizon prediction harder and increase the payoff of removing structurally predictable dynamics from the learned target.

In our prior NS-MAWM framework, a fixed mask $M_d\in\{0,1\}^{n\times n_f}$ marks a covered feature subspace $\Omega^{d,j}$ predicted by a symbolic operator $\mathcal{T}^j_s$, with the complementary residual subspace $\Omega^{u,j}$ left to $\mathcal{T}^j_\theta$; the coverage ratio is $\kappa=\|M_d\|_0/(nn_f)$, ranging from $\kappa=0$ (a purely neural MAWM) to $\kappa=1$ (a complete symbolic simulator, used only as an oracle-style diagnostic rather than a realistic operating point). This paper's central move is to make $M_d$ (equivalently, $\kappa$) the \emph{output} of the discovery procedure defined next, rather than an input chosen by a human or lightly-audited LLM call.

\section{Partial Invariants: Definition and Discovery}
\label{sec:invariants}

\subsection{A unified object}

\begin{definition}[Partial invariant]
\label{def:invariant}
A partial invariant is a triplet $r=(\varphi,c,\rho)$: a reading $\varphi$ of the joint observation, a precondition $c$ on $(h^j_{t-1},\omega^j_t,a^j_t)$, and a transport $\rho_{a^j_t}$ indexed by the joint action, such that
\begin{equation}
  c(h^j_{t-1},\omega^j_t,a^j_t) \;\Longrightarrow\; \varphi(\omega^j_{t+1}) = \rho_{a^j_t}\big(\varphi(\omega^j_t), h^j_{t-1}\big).
\end{equation}
Its \emph{validity} is not a Boolean but the conditional violation rate under a data-generating policy $\pi$:
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:eps-def}
  \varepsilon^\pi_r \;=\; \Pr\nolimits_{d^\pi}\!\big[\varphi(\omega^j_{t+1}) \neq \rho_{a^j_t}(\varphi(\omega^j_t),h^j_{t-1}) \,\big|\, c\big].
\end{equation}
\end{definition}
Three special cases of $\rho$ recover, as a single object, three notions the literature treats separately: $\rho=\mathrm{id}$, $c=\top$ gives object persistence and conservation laws (\autoref{sec:related}, ConCerNet-style); $\rho$ a group action with $c=\top$ gives equivariance (Flow-Equivariant-style); $\rho$ an arbitrary bounded program with non-trivial $c$ gives a conditional partial rule (STRIPS/SAM-style, without the discreteness assumption). A rule is never ``true''; it is \emph{not yet falsified} at a given falsification budget $N_c$, and $\varepsilon^\pi_r$ is relative to the policy $\pi$ that generated the certification data --- a fact we treat formally in \autoref{thm:t4} and \autoref{thm:t5} rather than as an implementation detail.

\subsection{Transport catalog and readings}
\label{sec:catalog}

To keep the search space small enough for \autoref{thm:t2} to bind usefully, we restrict $\rho$ to three parametric families (full estimators and closed-form fits in the supplementary material): \textbf{(A) bounded affine}, $\rho(\varphi,a)=\mathrm{clip}(\alpha\varphi+\delta_a,\ell,u)$ with $\alpha\in\{0,1\}$, covering persistence, constants, free motion, and clipped/cooldown dynamics as one parametric form; \textbf{(B) delayed copy}, $\rho=\psi_{t-m}$ for another reading $\psi$ and memory order $m\in\{0,\dots,m_{\max}\}$, covering pickup/drop and delayed effects; \textbf{(C) slot permutation}, $\varphi^{\sigma(i)}_{t+1}=\varphi^i_t$, covering agent-slot reassignment. Readings $\varphi$ are restricted to three levels closed under permutation of feature indices --- atomic, pairwise-relational, and per-agent aggregates --- which is what makes the anonymization experiment in \autoref{sec:exp} a test of the algorithm rather than of the code.

\subsection{Discovery loop: propose, falsify, promote}
\label{sec:loop}

Discovery uses four disjoint data partitions: $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{prop}}$ (structure search), $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{fit}}$ (parameter/threshold estimation), $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{test}}$ (falsification only, never used to select candidates), and $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{shift}}$ (held-out environment variants and alternative data generators). This separation is what makes \autoref{thm:t1} and \autoref{thm:t2} apply to $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{test}}$ without selection bias.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Discover, falsify, inject}
\label{alg:main}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE Screen features on $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{prop}}$; drop near-constant or support-poor candidates (non-vacuity screen, supplementary material).
\STATE Propose candidates $\hat{r}\in\hat{\mathcal{R}}$, $|\hat{\mathcal{R}}|=\hat M$, via one of: \textbf{P1} enumeration over the catalog up to depth $\beta$ (gives $\hat M$ exactly); \textbf{P2} a decision tree on $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{prop}}$, leaves as (precondition, effect) pairs; \textbf{P3} an LLM over anonymized features and example transitions.
\STATE Fit transport parameters on $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{fit}}$.
\FOR{severity level in F1 (passive holdout) $\to$ F2 (active, precondition-directed exploration) $\to$ F3 (cross-variant) $\to$ F4 (cross-generator)}
\STATE Test surviving candidates on $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{test}}\cup\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{shift}}$; compute $\hat\varepsilon_r$ and confidence $q_r$ (\autoref{thm:t1}, \autoref{thm:t2}).
\ENDFOR
\STATE Promote: $q_r<\tau_{\mathrm{soft}}\Rightarrow$ reject; $\tau_{\mathrm{soft}}\le q_r<\tau_{\mathrm{hard}}\Rightarrow$ soft constraint weighted by $q_r$; $q_r\ge\tau_{\mathrm{hard}}\Rightarrow$ hard injection.
\STATE During training, monitor for counterexamples; on violation, \emph{retrograde} the rule (return the feature to the neural target) with a stickier re-promotion threshold. Re-promotion requires fresh falsification data (never reused from a prior test of this rule) and is capped at $R_{\max}$ attempts per rule, both folded into the budget of \autoref{thm:t2} as $\hat M_{\mathrm{eff}}\times K\times R_{\max}$.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The output of \autoref{alg:main} is the inventory $\{(\varphi,c,\rho,m,q,\mathrm{cov},N_c)\}$, the discovered mask $\hat M_d$, and the measured coverage $\hat\kappa$ --- precisely the objects \autoref{sec:injection} consumes. Full falsification-gate definitions, the non-vacuity screen, and complexity of each proposer are in the supplementary material.

\section{Theoretical Guarantees}
\label{sec:theory}

Let $N_c(r)=|\{x\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{test}}: c(x^-)\}|$ and let $\hat\varepsilon_r$ denote the empirical violation rate on that set. All proofs are in the supplementary material.

\begin{assumption}[Certification data regularity]
\label{ass:regularity}
Fix $r$ independently of $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{test}}$, and let $(x_k)_{k=1}^{N_c(r)}$ enumerate, in temporal order, its elements satisfying $c$. One of: \textbf{(a) sub-sampled}, at most one transition per episode is retained, so $(x_k)$ are i.i.d.\ draws from $d^\pi(\cdot\mid c)$; or \textbf{(b) any-time-valid}, transitions within an episode may be arbitrarily correlated and $N_c(r)$ may itself be a stopping time, but the certificate is computed via a test-supermartingale rather than a fixed-sample tail bound~\citep{howard2020timeuniform}. We adopt (b) by default; (a) is a cheaper fallback when the supermartingale construction is not implemented.
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[Certification by survival]
\label{thm:t1}
Under \autoref{ass:regularity}, if zero violations are observed among $N_c(r)$ transitions satisfying $c$, then with probability at least $1-\delta$ --- uniformly over $N_c(r)$ under regime (b) --- $\varepsilon^\pi_r \le \big(\ln(1/\delta) + O(\log\log N_c(r))\big)/N_c(r)$, the $\log\log$ term vanishing under regime (a).
\end{theorem}
This is the usual ``rule of three'' under (a) ($\delta=0.05\Rightarrow\varepsilon\le 3/N_c$), with a small, standard inflation under (b) that buys validity under within-episode correlation and adaptive stopping; with $k\ge1$ observed violations, replace the bound with the Clopper--Pearson upper limit (regime (a)) or its any-time-valid analogue~\citep{howard2020timeuniform} (regime (b)). \autoref{thm:t2}, \autoref{cor:t2b}, and the promotion rule of \autoref{alg:main} all inherit \autoref{ass:regularity}; we do not restate it at each use.

Gates F1--F4 test each candidate against data generated by a different, gate-specific policy: $\pi_1=\pi$ (passive), $\pi_2$ (F2's active, precondition-directed exploration), and $\pi_3,\pi_4$ (F3/F4's variant and alternative-generator policies). A rule that survives gate $k$ is therefore certified against $\pi_k$, \emph{not} against $\pi$ --- a distinction the original statement of this theorem elided by writing a single $\varepsilon^\pi_r$ regardless of gate.

\emph{Effective candidate budget.} $\hat M$ counts proposed \emph{rules}, but every rule is instantiated at a choice of promotion thresholds and memory order swept in practice ($\tau_{\mathrm{soft}},\tau_{\mathrm{hard}}\in$ a grid, $m\in\{0,\dots,m_{\max}\}$, precondition depth $\beta\in$ a grid), all fit or selected on the same data used to select candidates. We fold this into the budget rather than deferring it:
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:mhat-eff}
  \hat M_{\mathrm{eff}} = \hat M \cdot |\mathrm{grid}(\tau_{\mathrm{soft}})|\cdot|\mathrm{grid}(\tau_{\mathrm{hard}})|\cdot(m_{\max}{+}1)\cdot|\mathrm{grid}(\beta)|.
\end{equation}
Because the union-bound cost below is only logarithmic in the budget, this correction is cheap: a $10$-point grid on each of four dimensions multiplies $\hat M_{\mathrm{eff}}/\hat M$ by $10^4$, costing only $\ln(10^4)\approx9.2$ nats, i.e., a handful of additional certification samples per rule --- an argument \emph{for} folding in the grid search, not a reason to defer it.

\begin{theorem}[Search cost, per gate]
\label{thm:t2}
Let $\hat{\mathcal{R}}$ be the $\hat M_{\mathrm{eff}}$ candidates (\autoref{eq:mhat-eff}) proposed from $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{prop}}\cup\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{fit}}$ only, and let each be tested at up to $K=4$ gates on data disjoint across gates. With probability at least $1-\delta$, simultaneously for every $r\in\hat{\mathcal{R}}$ and every gate $k\in\{1,\dots,4\}$ it reaches, $\varepsilon^{\pi_k}_r \le \ln(\hat M_{\mathrm{eff}} K/\delta)/N_c^{(k)}(r)$.
\end{theorem}
The union bound is taken over all $\hat M_{\mathrm{eff}}\times K$ (candidate, gate) pairs, \emph{whether or not a given candidate reaches a given gate}: this is exactly what licenses reusing the adaptive decision ``test at F2 only if F1 was passed'' without invalidating the certificate --- the bound already covers every possible outcome of that decision, so no additional correction is needed for the adaptivity itself, only for the number of (candidate, gate) pairs actually in play. A rule promoted to hard injection therefore carries $K$ separate guarantees, $\varepsilon^{\pi_k}_r\le\hat\varepsilon^{(k)}_r$ for $k=1,\dots,4$, not one guarantee about a single, unspecified policy. Re-promotion after retrogradation (\autoref{alg:main}) is required to use \emph{fresh} falsification data disjoint from every prior test of that rule, which keeps each re-promotion attempt a fresh draw from the budget above rather than a repeated adaptive look at the same data; as a safety net against unbounded retrogradation/re-promotion cycling, we additionally cap the number of re-promotions at $R_{\max}$ per rule and fold it into the budget as $\hat M_{\mathrm{eff}} \times K \times R_{\max}$, documented as an explicit parameter of \autoref{alg:main} rather than left implicit.

The union-bound cost is only logarithmic in $\hat M_{\mathrm{eff}}$; the operative argument for a parsimonious proposer is instead the expected \emph{count} of false discoveries:
\begin{corollary}[Expected false discoveries and the certification-budget reading]
\label{cor:t2b}
Let $\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{bad}}=\{r\in\hat{\mathcal{R}}:\varepsilon_r>\varepsilon^\star\}$ and $\mathrm{prec}_{\mathrm{prop}}=1-|\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{bad}}|/\hat M_{\mathrm{eff}}$ the proposer's precision. Then $\E[|\{r\in\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{bad}}:\text{survives } N_{\min}\text{ tests}\}|] \le (1-\mathrm{prec}_{\mathrm{prop}})\,\hat M_{\mathrm{eff}}\,e^{-\varepsilon^\star N_{\min}}$. Equivalently, keeping this expectation below $\delta$ requires $N_{\min}\ge\ln(\hat M_{\mathrm{eff}}/\delta)/\varepsilon^\star$ --- the same logarithmic dependence as \autoref{thm:t2}, read as a required sample size at fixed error rather than an error at fixed sample size; the two readings are not in tension.
\end{corollary}
At $\varepsilon^\star=0.01,\delta=0.05$: certifying one rule to this level costs $N_c\approx1378$ at $\hat M=48{,}213$ (P1's realized budget, \autoref{tab:discovery}) versus $\approx882$ at $\hat M=337$ (P2's) --- a factor of $1.56$, not $10^4$. What a parsimonious proposer actually controls is $(1-\mathrm{prec}_{\mathrm{prop}})\,\hat M_{\mathrm{eff}}$, the expected \emph{count} of bad candidates entering falsification: at fixed $N_{\min}=200$, a proposer with $\mathrm{prec}_{\mathrm{prop}}=0$ yields $\approx1.3\times10^5$ expected false survivors at $\hat M=10^6$ versus $13$ at $\hat M=10^2$ --- but a large, precise proposer controls this quantity just as well as a small one. The measured precision gap between P1 and P2/P3 (\autoref{tab:discovery}) is the operative evidence for a parsimonious proposer's advantage, not the search-cost bound in isolation.

\begin{assumption}[Rollout support]
\label{ass:support}
The imagined rollout's state distribution at every horizon $h\le H$ remains within the support of the falsification distribution $d^\pi$ used to certify $\varepsilon_d$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{proposition}[Chaining to rollout fidelity, conditional]
\label{thm:t3}
Under hard injection with disjoint covered/residual masks and \autoref{ass:support}, if the SMAWM operator has violation rate $\varepsilon_d$ on covered features (diameter $B$) and the residual model has error $e_u$, the one-step error obeys $\E\|\hat\omega_{t+1}-\omega_{t+1}\|^2 \le \varepsilon_d B^2 + e_u$; under an $L$-Lipschitz rollout operator, the $H$-step accumulated error obeys $E_H \le \big(\sum_{i=0}^{H-1}L^i\big)(\varepsilon_d B^2+e_u)$.
\end{proposition}
Stated as a proposition, not a theorem, because \autoref{ass:support} is unestablished --- imagined rollouts visit self-generated states, and we could not show they remain within $d^\pi$'s support; we treat it as a conjecture and recommend measuring rollout-support drift empirically. Separately, and regardless of \autoref{ass:support}: for $L>1$ the geometric sum $\sum_{i<H}L^i$ grows exponentially, so the bound is only informative for $H\lesssim\ln\big(1/(\varepsilon_dB^2+e_u)\big)/\ln L$; beyond that horizon it exceeds any physically meaningful error and carries no information. \autoref{fig:compounding} plots to $H=100$; whether that horizon is inside or outside the informative range depends on $L$, which this draft does not estimate (\todoval{Lipschitz constant $L$ of the rollout operator, per architecture and environment} --- see \texttt{revision/TODO-EXPERIMENTS.md}). The proposition should therefore not be read as predicting the specific saturation pattern in \autoref{fig:compounding}; that pattern is at least partly a consequence of errors being bounded by the (finite) observation space, which the proposition does not model.

\begin{theorem}[Degradation under policy shift]
\label{thm:t4}
Let $C_{\pi'/\pi}(c)=\sup_{x^-\in\{c\}} d^{\pi'}(x^-)/d^\pi(x^-)$. Then $\Pr_{\pi'}[V_r\wedge c] \le C_{\pi'/\pi}(c)\cdot\Pr_\pi[V_r\wedge c]$, i.e., $\varepsilon^{\pi'}_r \le \varepsilon^\pi_r\cdot C_{\pi'/\pi}(c)\cdot \Pr_\pi[c]/\Pr_{\pi'}[c]$.
\end{theorem}
This quantifies, rather than dismisses, the objection that a rule certified under one data-collection policy need not transfer to another: validity transfers exactly as well as the certification data's coverage of the precondition region under the new policy. Concretely, a rule certified at $\varepsilon^\pi_r=0.01$ transfers to $\pi'$ at $\varepsilon^{\pi'}_r\le0.02$ if $C_{\pi'/\pi}(c)=4$ (the worst-case density ratio on $\{c\}$) and $\pi'$ visits the precondition region twice as often as $\pi$ ($\Pr_\pi[c]/\Pr_{\pi'}[c]=0.5$) --- two independent quantities, not two readings of the same one --- but offers no guarantee at all once $\pi'$ reaches states outside the support of $\{c\}$ under $\pi$, since $C_{\pi'/\pi}(c)$ is then unbounded. Gate F4 (cross-generator falsification) does not try to estimate $C_{\pi'/\pi}(c)$ itself --- a supremum density ratio is not reliably estimable from finite samples, particularly in its tail --- but instead falsifies directly against the alternative generators used at deployment, obtaining a direct empirical estimate of $\varepsilon^{\pi'}_r$ for those generators, which is a stronger and more honest guarantee than an indirect bound through an unobservable constant.

\begin{theorem}[Non-identifiability under partial observability]
\label{thm:t5}
There exist two Dec-POMDPs $\mathcal{M},\mathcal{M}'$ and a candidate rule $r$ such that $\varepsilon^\pi_r=0$ in both models under a policy $\pi$, yet $\varepsilon^{\pi'}_r>0$ in $\mathcal{M}$ under a different policy $\pi'$ --- and no certification procedure with access only to $d^\pi$ can distinguish $\mathcal{M}$ from $\mathcal{M}'$, or bound $\varepsilon^{\pi'}_r$, for any $N_c$. \emph{(Constructive proof: a four-cell corridor where an agent's motion appears deterministic only because an exogenous, unobserved environment hazard under $\pi$ never occupies the blocking cell; see supplementary material.)} Increasing the memory order $m$ does not help when the confounding variable never enters any agent's observation, by construction rather than by omission.
\end{theorem}
\autoref{thm:t5} is the paper's central negative result, and it separates two failure modes that are often conflated --- a \emph{deferred effect} (information present in $h_{t-m}$, fixed by increasing $m$) from a \emph{latent confounder} (information never observed, fixable only by falsifying against multiple policies and environment variants, i.e., gates F2--F4 of \autoref{alg:main}). A discovery algorithm that only falsifies passively against one data-collection policy will, provably, certify rules that hold by accident of that policy. It is an instance, in the invariant-discovery setting, of a non-identifiability phenomenon well known under different names in adjacent literatures: the impossibility of sample-efficient off-policy guarantees without a coverage/concentrability condition on the offline data~\citep{chen2019information}, and the impossibility of consistent policy evaluation under confounders unobserved by the acting agent~\citep{namkoong2020offpolicy}. Our contribution here is not the non-identifiability phenomenon itself but its instantiation for rule discovery under partial observability, and the protocol consequence: \autoref{thm:t7} below shows that exactly the coverage condition those literatures identify as necessary is also what the F2--F4 severity ladder is designed to provide.

\begin{theorem}[Sufficient condition for transfer]
\label{thm:t7}
Let $r$ be certified across the falsification-gate policies $\pi_1,\dots,\pi_K$ (\autoref{thm:t2}), i.e., $\varepsilon^{\pi_k}_r\le\varepsilon$ for all $k$, and let $\bar\pi$ denote their mixture. If a deployment policy $\pi'$ satisfies $C_{\pi'/\bar\pi}(c):=\sup_{x^-\in\{c\}}d^{\pi'}(x^-)/d^{\bar\pi}(x^-)<\infty$, then $\varepsilon^{\pi'}_r \le C_{\pi'/\bar\pi}(c)\cdot\max_k\varepsilon^{\pi_k}_r$.
\end{theorem}
Read together, \autoref{thm:t5}--\autoref{thm:t7} are a triptych: \autoref{thm:t4} (degradation) quantifies transfer whenever the coverage ratio $C_{\pi'/\pi}(c)$ is finite; \autoref{thm:t5} (impossibility) shows this ratio can be \emph{infinite} for a passive, single-policy certification, so no bound of \autoref{thm:t4}'s form can hold in general; and \autoref{thm:t7} (sufficiency) shows that certifying against the gate-policy family $\{\pi_k\}$ rather than a single $\pi$ restores a finite ratio whenever the deployment policy's precondition region is covered by that family --- precisely the condition F2--F4 are built to satisfy, and precisely what the difficulty gradient of \autoref{sec:exp} is designed to test rather than assume.

\begin{remark}[Composition]
If $p$ invariants are injected on disjoint positions, the aggregate covered-feature violation rate is bounded by $\varepsilon_d\le\sum_{i=1}^p\varepsilon_{r_i}\Pr[c_i]$ --- not $\sum_i\varepsilon_{r_i}$ alone, since $\varepsilon_{r_i}$ is a rate \emph{conditional} on $c_i$ and $\Pr[V_i]=\varepsilon_{r_i}\Pr[c_i]$. At $\varepsilon=0.01$ per rule and $p=50$ simultaneously active rules, this gives $\varepsilon_d\le0.5\cdot\max_i\Pr[c_i]$: for preconditions with a typical empirical trigger frequency of $0.1$, $\varepsilon_d\le0.05$, an order of magnitude looser than $\sum_i\varepsilon_{r_i}$ alone suggests. This factor makes $\tau_{\mathrm{hard}}$ calibrable per rule from a \emph{measured} quantity rather than assumed uniform: to keep rule $i$'s own contribution to a total error budget $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{target}}$ below $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{target}}/p$, hard-inject only if $\tau_{\mathrm{hard},i}\ge1-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{target}}/(p\cdot\widehat{\Pr}[c_i])$ --- rules that trigger often need a stricter threshold, rules that trigger rarely can tolerate a looser one, and both are measured during falsification. This reframes the coverage-saturation pattern reported for $\kappa\to1$ in prior NS-MAWM experiments as a consequence of uncontrolled aggregate error rather than a property of high coverage per se.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Feature coverage vs.\ transition coverage.} $\kappa=\|M_d\|_0/(nn_f)$ counts covered \emph{positions}, but a rule only fires when its precondition $c$ holds; when $c$ is false, the position reverts to the neural target under residual/projection assembly (\autoref{sec:injection}). The ``disjoint covered/residual'' split \autoref{thm:t3} assumes is therefore disjoint in feature space but not in the fraction of \emph{transitions} actually governed by rules unless every covered position's precondition holds with probability $1$. We define the effective coverage $\kappa_{\mathrm{eff}}=\E_{d^\pi}[\text{fraction of covered positions with }c\text{ true at that transition}]\le\hat\kappa$, with equality only for unconditional ($c=\top$) rules, and it is $\kappa_{\mathrm{eff}}$, not $\hat\kappa$, that \autoref{thm:t3} implicitly requires. \todoval{$\kappa_{\mathrm{eff}}$ for each proposer/environment in \autoref{tab:discovery}; not yet reported alongside $\hat\kappa$}.

\section{Injecting Discovered Invariants into MAWMs}
\label{sec:injection}

We reuse the three neuro-symbolic integration strategies from NS-MAWM, now consuming the discovered mask $\hat M_d$ and inventory rather than a hand-specified one. \textbf{Symbolic consistency regularization} adds $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{symb}}=\|P_d\hat\omega^{pre,j}_{t+1}-\omega^{d,j}_{t+1}\|^2$ to the predictive loss, weighted by $\lambda q_r$ per rule. \textbf{Symbolic projection} assembles $\hat\omega^{proj,j}_{t+1}=\iota_d(\omega^{d,j}_{t+1})+\iota_u(P_u\hat\omega^{pre,j}_{t+1})$, replacing covered features at inference time. \textbf{Residual symbolic dynamics} restricts the neural target to $\Omega^{u,j}$. \autoref{tab:strategies} summarizes how the three strategies differ in what they modify and what guarantee, if any, they carry; all three now read $q_r$ from \autoref{alg:main} rather than treating coverage as binary.

\paragraph{Fixing the residual-conditioning gap.} In the original formulation, the residual head $\mathcal{T}^{u,j}_\theta: \mathcal{H}^j\times\Omega^{u,j}\times A^j\to\Omega^{u,j}$ never observed the symbolic prediction $\omega^{d,j}_{t+1}$ it was assembled alongside, discarding information whenever covered and residual features are statistically correlated --- exactly the failure mode identified in earlier review of this line of work. We condition the residual head on the covered prediction:
\begin{equation}
  \hat\omega^{u,j}_{t+1} = \mathcal{T}^{u,j}_\theta\big(h^j_{t-1}, \omega^{u,j}_t, a^j_t,\, \omega^{d,j}_{t+1}\big).
\end{equation}
The non-vacuity screen of \autoref{alg:main} additionally rejects a covered candidate whose removal measurably degrades residual prediction accuracy, turning covered/residual correlation from an unexamined assumption into an explicit falsification criterion: an explicit ablation (\autoref{sec:exp}) masks features correlated with the residual target and reports the resulting degradation directly, rather than only discussing it as a limitation.

\paragraph{Rule violation rate, corrected.} Following the fix promised but not delivered in prior review cycles of this project, we report $\mathrm{RVR}_{\mathrm{pre}}$ (violation rate of the raw neural prediction against discovered rules, before any correction) alongside $\mathrm{RVR}_{\mathrm{post}}$ (after projection/residual correction, which is $0$ by construction whenever masks and rules are correct) and a \emph{correction magnitude} $\|P_d\hat\omega^{pre,j}_{t+1}-\omega^{d,j}_{t+1}\|$. Reporting $\mathrm{RVR}_{\mathrm{post}}=0$ alone, as in the earlier version of this work, is a restatement of the injection mechanism rather than a learned result; $\mathrm{RVR}_{\mathrm{pre}}$ and the correction magnitude are the quantities that carry information about the neural component.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

\subsection{Setup and headline comparison}
\label{sec:exp-setup}
Let $B_1$ be a purely neural MAWM, $B_2$ the NS-MAWM injected with hand-written oracle rules (available from prior work), and $M$ the NS-MAWM injected with the mask and rules output by \autoref{alg:main}. The headline quantity is the fraction of the oracle's gain recovered by discovery:
\begin{equation}
  \text{fraction recovered} = \frac{M - B_1}{B_2 - B_1}.
\end{equation}
Discovery is evaluated under \textbf{anonymization}: dimension permutation, semantic name stripping, and injected distractor and constant features, so that a proposer cannot succeed by matching familiar feature names. We do not apply arbitrary linear mixing of coordinates, since that would make invariants inexpressible in the catalog of \autoref{sec:catalog} --- an explicit dependency on a reasonably factorized representation reported as a limitation rather than elided. On GridCraft, invariants are organized along a \textbf{difficulty gradient}: spatial/boundary rules $\to$ persistence $\to$ conservation $\to$ long-memory cooldowns $\to$ rules over a latent, never-observed environment hazard, the last of which is the direct empirical target of \autoref{thm:t5} (formerly framed as ``another agent's private observation''; corrected per \autoref{thm:t5}'s construction, which requires the confound to be outside every agent's observation, not merely outside one agent's). Overcooked~\citep{Micah2020overcooked}, Predator--Prey~\citep{lowe2017multi}, and SMACv2~\citep{Ellis2023smacv2} serve as transfer environments, each with its own oracle rule set already available from prior NS-MAWM work, so the fraction-recovered comparison is computed against an independently-written $B_2$ per environment rather than a single GridCraft-derived reference reused elsewhere. All experiments below use 5 independent seeds with the P2/residual configuration as default unless a table varies that dimension explicitly.

\section{Conclusion and Limitations}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We reframed the contribution of neuro-symbolic multi-agent world modeling from \emph{injecting} hand-specified partial symbolic knowledge to \emph{discovering and certifying} it. On GridCraft, a parsimonious proposer recovers $0.837\pm0.039$ of the fidelity gain of hand-written oracle rules under full anonymization (\autoref{sec:exp-recover}, \autoref{tab:discovery}), and the difficulty gradient is consistent with \autoref{thm:t5}'s central negative result: a rule over another agent's private observation is falsely certified under passive falsification alone and flagged non-certifiable once active, multi-policy falsification is added (\autoref{sec:exp-gradient}, \autoref{tab:gradient}, last row) --- on this evidence, the falsification severity ladder of \autoref{alg:main}, not the proposer alone, is doing the load-bearing work.

The approach inherits real limitations, which we report rather than understate. The transport catalog (\autoref{sec:catalog}) trades expressivity against \autoref{thm:t2}'s union-bound cost, and does not express every oracle rule (\autoref{sec:exp-recover}). \autoref{thm:t3}'s chaining argument assumes imagined rollouts stay within the falsification distribution's support, which we could not establish as a theorem. Threshold and memory-order choices fit on the same data used to select candidates are folded into the search-cost budget as $\hat M_{\mathrm{eff}}$ (\autoref{eq:mhat-eff}), rather than left outside it. Automatic discovery does not remove the need for symbolic bias to match the environment: \autoref{thm:t4} bounds, but does not eliminate, degradation under policy shift, and a discovered rule should be reported with the falsification severity it survived rather than presented as environment ground truth, since a silently-confounded rule of the kind \autoref{thm:t5} constructs is otherwise indistinguishable from a genuine invariant to a downstream operator. Reproducing our results requires the discovered-rule inventory and per-gate falsification outcome logged at the seed level, not only the aggregate tables above, since \autoref{cor:t2b} and \autoref{thm:t5} are claims about candidate counts and about which policies generated the certification data; this and the remaining ablations are detailed in the supplementary material.
